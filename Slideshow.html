Introduction

Stochastic Monte Carlo Model to simulate the probability of a susceptible person catching an airborne disease.

Article

Beggs. et al., a study conducted to understand the harm infectious people bring to healthcare waiting rooms. Their hope was to to have findings that backed up the idea to have more precautions to minimize the spread of airborne infections. The diseases they looked at were Tuberculosis, Measles, and Influenza.

In our article, the researchers attempt to predict the infection rate of an airborne disease when waiting in a waiting room. They did their study on Tuberculous, Measles, and Influenza. They had had specific requirements of room size, ventilation rate, …

Assumptions

  • Only one infected person in the waiting room
  • Once infected, the person can not infect others
  • The air being ventilated through the room is mixture of outside air and “infected” air (not being filtered)

Scenario 1

For the first scenario, the factors we changed were:

  • room size has an increased size
  • exposure time is longer by 30 seconds (1 minute in total).
  • only one infected person in the room.

Prediction: the infection probability would decrease.
