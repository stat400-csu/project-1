Introduction

Stochastic Monte Carlo Model to simulate the probability of a susceptible person catching an airborne disease.

Article

Beggs. et al., a study conducted to understand the harm infectious people bring to healthcare waiting rooms. Their hope was to to have findings that backed up the idea to have more precautions to minimize the spread of airborne infections. The diseases they looked at were Tuberculosis, Measles, and Influenza.

Assumptions they made: - Only one infectious individual present in each simulation - Waiting room is ventilated w outside air at a constant flow rate - Air in waiting room is mixed so infectious particles are evenly distributed throughout the room space. - values of p, phi, t, and S vary and are normally distributed. - applied an SD of 25% of the mean quanta production val. - Once susceptible individuals become infected they cannot infect anyone else (bc they leave within the 30min they are there.)

Assumptions

  • Only one infected person in the waiting room

Replication

We had 2 main goals going into this project:

  • we wanted to observe probability of infection as we changed some of Beggs et al. set variables such as room volume, number of ppl infected, and exposure time
  • we wanted to replicate their Monte Carlo approach and apply it to a different disease (SARS)
